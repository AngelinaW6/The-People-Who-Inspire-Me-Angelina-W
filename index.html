The People Who Inspire Me

B.J. Habibie

Bacharuddin Jusuf Habibie (B.J. Habibie) was the third President of Indonesia (1998–1999), a brilliant aerospace engineer, and globally recognized scientist. Born on June 25, 1936, in Parepare, South Sulawesi, he earned the title "Father of Technology" for his vital contributions to aviation and national science. He passed away on September 11, 2019, in Jakarta.

BJ Habibie inspires me because he showed how a person from Indonesia can master advanced aerospace engineering and build real aircraft. He proved that hard work and a brilliant mind can conquer the hardest global scientific challenges. His deep love for his late wife, Hasri Ainun Besari, taught us the true meaning of lifelong devotion. He returned to his home country to serve as president during a tough time and led important democratic changes. Habibie valued education and pushed young people to study science and technology. His visionary ideas helped transform local industries and shaped a modern national identity. He always remained humble and kept a joyful, warm spirit despite his immense fame. His journey teaches us to believe in our own dreams and never give up. He proved that integrity matters more than power in public service. Ultimately, his remarkable life shows us how to love our country through real, lasting contributions.

Raden Ajeng Kartini was a national hero from Indonesia and a pioneer in women's rights. She was born on April 21, 1879, in Jepara, Central Java. She fought hard for equal education and freedom for women. Her famous letters were made into a book named "From Darkness to Light".

Raden Adjeng Kartini inspires me because she fought bravely for women's rights and equality in Indonesia during a strict time. She believed that education is the key to freedom for all women. Her deep letters show a strong mind that wanted to change old rules. She dreamed of a world where girls could learn and grow just like boys. Kartini opened a school to teach local girls basic skills and knowledge. She proved that gentle hands can also hold powerful and wise ideas. Her bold vision broke the dark walls of traditional limits. She gave a voice to the voiceless and hope to future generations. I feel strong when I read her words about chasing big dreams. Kartini remains a bright light who shows us how to be brave and kind.

Wilhem Wundt

Wilhelm Wundt (1832–1920) was a German physiologist, philosopher, and professor known as the "father of experimental psychology." He separated psychology from philosophy by opening the first experimental psychology laboratory at the University of Leipzig in 1879.

Wilhelm Wundt inspires because he founded the first psychological laboratory in 1873, turning the mind into a science. He proved that human thoughts and feelings can be measured through controlled experiments. His focus on introspection showed that self-reflection is a valid path to understanding consciousness. Wundt trained many brilliant students who spread psychology across the entire world. He separated psychology from philosophy and physiology to give it a unique identity. His massive book, Principles of Physiological Psychology, laid the structural framework for modern research. He believed that even complex mental processes break down into basic sensory elements. Wundt viewed humans as active processors of experience rather than passive observers. His scientific courage encourages modern thinkers to question how the mind works. Ultimately, his lifelong dedication reminds us that studying human experience requires both passion and systematic proof.
